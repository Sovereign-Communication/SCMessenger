## What the verdict caught, and what changed

The reviewer was right and your ruling-admission was right: the first gate
authenticated a **wire-supplied peer id**, not the address -- so an attacker
could bind any address to a verified identity (Bypass A), and wire pids could
be written onto locally_verified entries (Bypass B). The rework implements the
corrected requirement exactly as you wrote it.

## The corrected gate

`LedgerManager::is_locally_verified_pair(addr, peer_id)` -- ADDRESS-keyed
(`entry_for_multiaddr`), requiring: entry exists for this exact stripped
address; `locally_verified` (only writer: `record_connection`); the
export-equivalent health tier (`success_count > 0`,
`failure_count < THRESHOLD`); current `peer_id` binding matches (canonicalized
both ways). Never consults `observed_peer_ids`; never accepts a wire-supplied
pid as the key. The wire may NAME the pair; our store must PROVE it.
